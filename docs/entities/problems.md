| breath-stopping-anxiety | Breath Stops, or Won't Behave — Should I Worry? | During sitting my breathing sometimes becomes very shallow or seems to pause by itself, or I find myself holding or fighting the breath. What is this, and is it safe? | The tradition distinguishes two things that feel similar. The spontaneous version — breath becoming fine and nearly imperceptible, without effort, comfortable, reversible — is the classical ideal in its earliest form: the Baopuzi's taixi, 'breathing without nose or mouth,' and the Xiao Zhiguan's 'as if present, as if absent.' The forced version — holding the breath, racing it, or feeling it fought — is the manuals' named fault (the 'wind' breath), and the manuals explicitly warn against foods and efforts that make the qi 'strong and hard to close.' If breathing difficulty comes with distress, chest symptoms, or breathlessness outside sitting, it is a medical matter, not a meditation matter. |

| drowsiness-in-sitting | Why do I get drowsy or fall asleep during sitting — and is that stillness? | Why do I get drowsy or fall asleep during sitting — and is drowsiness the same as entering stillness? | No. The manuals are explicit: drowsiness (昏沉) is an error state, and mistaking it for stillness (入静) is a named beginner's mistake. Stillness is lucid and light; drowsiness is dull and heavy. The standard causes are heavy meals, collapsed posture, and sitting while exhausted — and the remedies are direct: eat lightly, hold the posture, rest before sitting. |

| restlessness-in-sitting | Is restlessness or discomfort in sitting a sign I should stop? | When sitting feels restless, tight, or unpleasant — should I push through or stop? | The earliest Chinese protocol says: stop. The Taiping Jing instructs leaving the chamber when restless or unwell and explicitly says not to force the practice. Forcing is counterproductive in these sources; endurance is not the method. |
| seeing-light-in-sitting | Seeing Light or Images During Sitting — What Does It Mean? | During sitting I sometimes see light, colors, or images. Is this progress, illusion, or a warning sign? | The phenomenon is named in the earliest sources — the Zhuangzi's 'the empty room gives rise to white light' (虚室生白) — and the tradition's counsel across manuals is consistent: notice without grasping, neither chase nor fear.
