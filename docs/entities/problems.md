| body-feels-very-large | Body Feels Very Large During Sitting — What Is That? | Why does my body feel very large, swollen, or boundaryless during sitting? | The tradition has a name for the felt edge of this: the Zhuangzi's 'heaven and earth were born together with me, and the myriad things and I are one' — the felt boundary of the body loosening in stillness is an old and documented report, not damage. | breath-retention-daoist | Is Breath Retention Part of Daoist Meditation? | Is breath retention part of Daoist meditation? | No — the classical texts point the other way. The Daoist ideal is taixi, 'embryonic breathing': breath so fine and continuous it seems to stop, explicitly framed as an arrival after long practice, not a technique of holding. The texts attach conditions of softness and diet to it, and no classical manual this site covers teaches forced breath retention; attempts to force it are the documented source of the fear described on the breath-stopping page. |
| breath-stopping-anxiety | Breath Stops, or Won't Behave — Should I Worry? | During sitting my breathing sometimes becomes very shallow or seems to pause by itself, or I find myself holding or fighting the breath. What is this, and is it safe? | The tradition distinguishes two things that feel similar. The spontaneous version — breath becoming fine and nearly imperceptible, without effort, comfortable, reversible — is the classical ideal in its earliest form: the Baopuzi's taixi, 'breathing without nose or mouth,' and the Xiao Zhiguan's 'as if present, as if absent.' The forced version — holding the breath, racing it, or feeling it fought — is the manuals' named fault (the 'wind' breath), and the manuals explicitly warn against foods and efforts that make the qi 'strong and hard to close.' If breathing difficulty comes with distress, chest symptoms, or breathlessness outside sitting, it is a medical matter, not a meditation matter. |
| breath-stopping-intentional-spontaneous | Is the Breath Stopping Intentional or Spontaneous? | Is the breath stopping during meditation intentional or spontaneous? | In the tradition's own terms, spontaneous — the fine breath of deep stillness arrives, and the manuals never teach commanding it. The archive records the same shape from the practitioner side: the stoppage that came by itself was welcomed; the stoppage that was chased produced anxiety. The rule follows: never force the breath; the sensation of needing air is a signal to breathe, and breath discomfort is a medical boundary. |
| breathing-gets-shallow | Breathing Becomes Very Shallow in Sitting — Problem or Progress? | Why does my breathing become very shallow during sitting? | A quieting body breathes less loudly — the tradition's breath hierarchy runs from the faults (wind, panting, coarse) down to the ideal, breath 'as if present, as if absent.' Slow, shallow breath in stillness is the direction of travel, and modern slow-breathing research describes its physiology (parasympathetic predominance at under ten breaths per minute). The boundary is the sensation of needing air: fine breath never includes air hunger, and air hunger or breath discomfort is a medical matter. |
| dizziness-during-sitting | Dizziness During or After Sitting — Breathing, Blood, and the Stop Rule | Why do I feel dizzy during or after sitting meditation? | Dizziness in sitting has two honest readings. It can come from the breath work itself: over-breathing lowers the blood's carbon dioxide, which narrows the brain's blood vessels — lightheadedness is the classic sign. Or it can come from the body's ordinary states around practice: rising too quickly after a long sit, sitting on an empty stomach, a held neck posture. The site's archive contains one explicit rule, and it is blunt: stop at the first dizziness. No text in this site's corpus describes dizziness as a stage or a sign of progress, and persistent or recurrent dizziness is a medical matter. |
| drowsiness-in-sitting | Why do I get drowsy or fall asleep during sitting — and is that stillness? | Why do I get drowsy or fall asleep during sitting — and is drowsiness the same as entering stillness? | No. The manuals are explicit: drowsiness (昏沉) is an error state, and mistaking it for stillness (入静) is a named beginner's mistake. Stillness is lucid and light; drowsiness is dull and heavy. The standard causes are heavy meals, collapsed posture, and sitting while exhausted — and the remedies are direct: eat lightly, hold the posture, rest before sitting. |
| falling-asleep-during-sitting | Falling Asleep During Sitting — What the Tradition Says | Why do I fall asleep during sitting meditation? | The tradition knows the failure mode well: stillness and sleepiness are neighbors, and the manuals answer with posture, timing, and alertness — not with guilt. An under-rested body falls asleep in stillness exactly as it would in a lecture chair, and the archives record the same arc (sleep early on, stability later). The medical boundary is the same as the drowsiness page's: persistent daytime sleepiness is a sleep-medicine matter. |
| forgetting-the-body-daoist-texts | What Does 'Forgetting the Body' Mean in Daoist Texts? | What does 'forgetting the body' (忘身 / 堕肢体) mean in Daoist texts? | In Zhuangzi ch. 6, 'dropping away the limbs and body' is the first item in the definition of sitting-and-forgetting, and it means the felt solidity of the body relaxing its claim on attention — not harm to the body and not loss of contact with reality. The word 'forget' (忘) is doing precise work: forgetting a thing one still has, the way one forgets shoes when they fit well (Zhuangzi ch. 19). |
| head-pressure-during-sitting | Pressure or Tightness in the Head During Sitting | Why do I feel pressure or tightness in my head during sitting? | Head pressure in sitting is reported in the site's archive, and the two available readings agree on the practical rule: ease off. The archive teacher read head swelling after breath practice as a blood-pressure rise and advised easing off the breath work and adding exercise; the modern reading points to the tension-headache pattern — sustained contraction of scalp and neck muscles producing band-like pressure. No text in this site's corpus describes head pressure as a sign of progress, and new or worsening head pain is a medical matter. |
| losing-body-awareness | Losing Awareness of the Body During Sitting — Named and Expected? | Why do I lose awareness of my body during sitting? | The earliest sources name it: the Zhuangzi's sitting-and-forgetting includes 'dropping away the limbs and body, expelling perception' — the felt body receding is a described stage of the practice, not a malfunction. The tradition means a *quieting* of body-claim, not loss of contact with reality, and the boundary is sharp: if the experience frightens you, continues off the cushion, or feels like depersonalization, it is a medical matter. |
| restlessness-in-sitting | Is restlessness or discomfort in sitting a sign I should stop? | When sitting feels restless, tight, or unpleasant — should I push through or stop? | The earliest Chinese protocol says: stop. The Taiping Jing instructs leaving the chamber when restless or unwell and explicitly says not to force the practice. Forcing is counterproductive in these sources; endurance is not the method. |

| stillness-uncomfortable | Stillness Feels Uncomfortable — Why Is Quiet Hard? | Why does stillness feel uncomfortable when I sit? | Because stillness removes the noise that covers the mind's racing — and a mind that races finds quiet uncomfortable, the way silence makes a ringing ear audible. The Zhuangzi already names this as the condition practice begins from ('the body sits, the mind gallops'), so early discomfort in quiet is the expected starting point, not a sign of failure. The boundary: discomfort that is physical pain or emotional distress is a different matter, and each has its own page. |
| thoughts-become-stronger | Thoughts Become Stronger When I Sit — Is That Backwards? | Why do thoughts become stronger and louder when I start to meditate? | The tradition's answer is that stillness does not add thoughts — it removes the noise that covered them. What feels like 'stronger thinking' is usually hearing what was always running. This is the expected starting condition, not a failure; the practice is the slow settling of it, and the only medical boundary is when thought content itself is distressing or intrusive. |
| vibrations-during-sitting | Vibrations or Shaking During Sitting — What Does the Tradition Say? | Why do I feel vibrations, buzzing, or shaking during sitting? | The traditional frame for movement felt inside the body is qi-movement, and the manuals' rule is the site-wide one: observe without grasping. Muscle tremor as stillness deepens has ordinary physiological explanations (postural muscles settling, a tired body unclenching), but no research exists on vibration sensations in Daoist sitting, and persistent tremor or one-sided shaking is a medical matter. |
